## Intent

- Name the two mechanisms of core loss: hysteresis and eddy currents.
- Estimate the core loss per unit volume with the Steinmetz equation from the flux amplitude and the frequency.
- Explain why, at high switching frequencies, core loss rather than saturation limits the flux swing.

## Theory

### Hysteresis and eddy currents

In every period the flux density traces the core's B-H loop, and the energy
enclosed by the loop is lost as heat: the hysteresis loss
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. The changing flux also induces
currents in the core material itself, which dissipate power in its
resistance: the eddy-current loss <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
Steinmetz found that the hysteresis loss per cycle grows with a power of the
peak flux density <Cite key="steinmetz1984" />.

### Why the swing is limited at high frequency

At a fixed voltage and duty ratio, the flux swing is inversely proportional
to the switching frequency. At high switching frequencies the swing that
keeps the core loss acceptable is usually well below the saturation flux
density, so core loss, not saturation, sets the flux swing in a transformer
design <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 12" />.

Sweep $B_\mathrm{ac}$ on its logarithmic axis: on a log-log plot the loss is
a straight line whose slope is the exponent $\beta$. Halving the flux
amplitude divides the loss by two to the power $\beta$.

## Bench exercise

Drive a transformer's primary with a square wave, secondary open, and record
the primary voltage and current with an oscilloscope. The average of their
product over a period is the core loss plus a small winding loss. Deskew the
voltage and current probes first: the magnetizing current is mostly
reactive, so a small timing error changes the result a lot. Repeat at half
the voltage and compare the ratio of the losses with two to the power $\beta$.

## Gotchas

- **The coefficients are empirical.** They hold only for the material, the
  temperature, and the range of frequency and flux density over which they
  were fitted <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
- **Use half the peak-to-peak swing.** $B_\mathrm{ac}$ is the amplitude, half of
  $\Delta B$; using the whole swing overstates the loss by two to the power
  $\beta$.
- **A filter inductor with small ripple has a small swing.** Its core loss is
  then modest, and the dc flux, which causes no core loss, sets its
  saturation limit <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
