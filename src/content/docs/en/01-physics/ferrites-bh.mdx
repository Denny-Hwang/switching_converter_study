## Intent

- Relate flux density to field intensity through the permeability.
- Explain saturation, and why an inductor must be checked at its peak current.
- Compute an inductor's peak flux density from its inductance, peak current, turns and core area.

## Theory

### Permeability

In a linear magnetic material the flux density is proportional to the field
intensity. The factor is the permeability, written as the permeability of
free space times the relative permeability of the material

### Saturation and hysteresis

The linear relation holds only up to the saturation flux density. Beyond it
the material's permeability collapses toward that of free space, and an
inductor's inductance falls sharply <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
The B-H curve of a real core also shows hysteresis: going up and coming
down, the flux density follows different paths, and the area between them is
energy lost in every cycle (see the [core-loss page](../core-loss/))
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.

### Ferrites and iron alloys

Ferrites saturate at a lower flux density than iron alloys, but their high
resistivity keeps eddy-current loss low, which suits them to high switching
frequencies <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.

### Peak flux density of an inductor

An inductor's flux linkage is its inductance times its current; divided by
the turns and the core area, it gives the flux density. At the peak current

## Bench exercise

Discharge a charged capacitor into the inductor through a transistor for a
fixed time, and watch the inductor current with a current probe. While the
core is linear the current ramps in a straight line; when the core
saturates, the slope rises sharply. Read the current at the knee, compare it
with the peak-flux equation and the data-sheet saturation flux density, and
limit the pulse length so that the current stays within the parts' ratings.

## Gotchas

- **Saturation is abrupt.** Past saturation the inductance collapses and the
  current rises steeply <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />; design for
  the worst-case peak current, not the average.
- **A core's permeability is not a precise number.** With a gap, the
  inductance depends mostly on the gap length instead of the material
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" /> (see the [gap page](../gap-and-al/)).
