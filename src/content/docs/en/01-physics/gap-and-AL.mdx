## Intent

- Compute the inductance factor of a gapped core from its dimensions, and the inductance from the turns.
- Explain why a gap makes the inductance depend on the gap rather than on the core material.
- Explain how a gap lowers the flux density that an inductor reaches at a given current.

## Theory

### Magnetic circuits

A magnetic path behaves like a resistive circuit: the winding's
magnetomotive force, its turns times its current, drives the flux through
reluctances in series, here the core's and the gap's
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. The core's reluctance is its path
length divided by its permeability and area; the gap's is the gap length
divided by the permeability of free space and the area. Because the core's
relative permeability is large, even a short gap can dominate the total.

### The inductance factor

The inductance per turn squared, the inductance factor $A_L$, is the inverse
of the total reluctance <Cite key="dixon2001" />,

When the gap term dominates, $A_L$ depends on the gap length and the area and
hardly on $\mu_i$: the gap makes the inductance insensitive to the spread of
the core material <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.

### Why power inductors are gapped

The core material saturates at a given flux density, and a gap does not
change that limit. It lowers the inductance factor, however, so a given
inductance needs more turns, and at a given current the flux density falls
(<EqRef id="mag.B_pk" label="peak flux density" />): the gapped inductor
carries more current before it saturates.

## Bench exercise

Measure the inductance of a winding on a pair of ungapped ferrite core
halves. Then place thin non-magnetic spacers of known thickness, such as
plastic film, between the halves and measure again. Compare the change with
the equation, counting every place where the flux path crosses a spacer.
The measured inductance is somewhat larger than predicted, because the flux
fringes around the gap <Cite key="dixon2001" />.

## Gotchas

- **Fringing makes the real $A_L$ larger.** The flux bulges out around the
  gap, which widens its effective area, so the simple equation underestimates
  the inductance <Cite key="dixon2001" />.
- **Use the manufacturer's $A_L$ for a specified gap.** The equation serves to
  understand a gap and to estimate a custom one. Because fringing is hard to
  calculate, a catalogue core with a specified gap is better described by the
  manufacturer's measured $A_L$.
- **Turns multiply as a square.** Doubling the turns on the same core
  quadruples the inductance.
