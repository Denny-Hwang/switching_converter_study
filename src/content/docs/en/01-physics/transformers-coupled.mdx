## Intent

- Apply the ideal-transformer relations with the site's 1:n turns-ratio convention.
- Add the magnetizing and leakage inductances to model a real transformer.
- Tell a transformer, which passes energy through, from a coupled inductor, which stores it.

## Theory

### The ideal transformer

Two windings on one core, with perfect coupling, no magnetizing current and
no loss, form an ideal transformer. With the turns ratio written 1:n, where
$n$ is the number of secondary turns divided by the number of primary turns,
the voltage is multiplied by $n$ and the current divided by $n$
<Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 13" />, <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />:

The power is conserved, and an impedance connected to the secondary appears
at the primary divided by $n^2$ <Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 13" />.

### Magnetizing inductance

A real core has a finite permeability, so it takes a current to set up the
flux, even with the secondary open. This magnetizing current is modelled by
an inductance $L_M$ in parallel with the primary of an ideal transformer,
referred to the primary <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />. It grows
while a voltage is applied to the winding, which is why a transformer must be
reset in every period (see the [forward converter](../../03-topologies/forward/)).

### Leakage inductance

Not all of the flux links both windings. The flux that links only one of
them appears as a leakage inductance in series with that winding
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. It slows the transfer of current
from one winding to the other, and in a flyback its energy must be absorbed
at every turn-off (see the [flyback page](../../03-topologies/flyback/)).

### Transformer or coupled inductor

A transformer transfers energy from the primary to the secondary while both
windings conduct, and ideally stores none. A flyback's coupled inductor works
differently: its magnetizing inductance stores energy while the primary
conducts and releases it through the secondary afterwards
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />. The two are therefore designed for
different goals: the transformer for a small magnetizing current, the coupled
inductor for a specified magnetizing inductance and stored energy
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.

## Bench exercise

Drive the primary of a small signal transformer from a function generator
with a sine wave within its rated frequency range, with the secondary open.
Measure both winding voltages to find the turns ratio. Then measure the
primary current with the secondary open, which is the magnetizing current,
and with the secondary shorted, which is limited by the leakage inductance,
and estimate both inductances from the voltage, current and frequency.

## Gotchas

- **Check the turns-ratio convention.** Data sheets and textbooks write turns
  ratios in different directions; this site always uses 1:n with $n$ the
  secondary turns divided by the primary turns.
- **The magnetizing current is not load current.** It flows with no load at
  all, and the net volt-seconds on the winding must be zero in every period,
  or the magnetizing current grows until the core saturates
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />.
- **Mind the dots.** Reversing one winding inverts the secondary voltage; in a
  flyback the winding polarity is chosen so that the diode blocks while the
  switch is on <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />.
