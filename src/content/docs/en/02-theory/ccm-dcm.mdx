## Intent

- Explain why the discontinuous conduction mode (DCM) appears at light load when the second switch is a diode.
- Compute the diode-conduction interval and the DCM conversion ratio of the buck, boost and buck-boost.
- Recognise DCM on an oscilloscope from its waveforms.

## Theory

### Where DCM comes from

In the basic converters the second switch is a diode, which conducts in one
direction only. The inductor current is a dc value plus a triangular ripple.
When the load gets lighter, the dc value falls but the ripple does not: in
CCM the ripple depends only on the applied voltages, $L$ and $T_s$. Once the
lower peak of the ripple reaches zero, the diode stops conducting before the
period ends, and the inductor current stays at zero until the switch turns
on again <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />. Each period now has three
subintervals: the switch conducts for $D T_s$, the diode for $D_2 T_s$, and
neither for the remaining $D_3 T_s$. This is the **discontinuous conduction
mode (DCM)**. The normal case, in which the inductor current never reaches
zero, is the **continuous conduction mode (CCM)**.

Which mode a converter runs in is decided by the dimensionless parameter
<EqRef id="K.def" label="K" /> and its critical value; see the
[K-parameter page](../k-parameter/).

### Analysing DCM

Volt-second balance and charge balance still hold, but two things change
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />:

1. The diode interval $D_2$ is a new unknown, so a second equation is needed.
2. The inductor current ripple is no longer small compared with its dc value,
   so the small-ripple approximation must not be applied to the inductor
   current. Its average is computed from the actual triangular waveform.

### What changes in DCM

- **The conversion ratio depends on the load.** In CCM, $M$ is a function
  of $D$ alone; in DCM it also depends on $K$, and so on $R$. As the load is
  removed, $K$ goes to zero: the buck output rises towards $V_g$, and the
  boost and buck-boost outputs rise without bound, as the equations above
  show. An open-loop converter must therefore never run without a load.
- **The peak inductor current no longer depends on the load.** Each period
  starts from zero current, so the peak is set by the voltage applied during
  $D T_s$, by $L$ and by $T_s$.
- **The switch node rings during the idle interval.** A real switch node has
  capacitance, from the switch and the diode. When the inductor current
  reaches zero, that capacitance and the inductor form a lightly damped L-C
  circuit, which rings at

Sweep $K$ over several decades (logarithmic axis). At small $K$ the ratio
approaches 1. As $K$ grows the ratio falls, and it equals the CCM value $D$
exactly where $K$ reaches $K_\mathrm{crit}$. Beyond that point the converter
is in CCM and this formula no longer applies, although the explorer keeps
plotting it.

## Bench exercise

Take a buck converter with a diode rectifier, such as a low-voltage
evaluation board with an asynchronous controller in fixed-frequency mode,
and a resistive load you can vary. Watch the inductor current with a
current probe, and the switch node with a voltage probe, while you lighten
the load. Note the load at which the current first touches zero, the
appearance of the idle interval, and the ringing on the switch node during
it. Check that, with the duty ratio held fixed, the output voltage rises as
the load gets lighter.

## Gotchas

- **Synchronous rectification removes DCM.** DCM arises because the diode
  cannot carry reverse current. A transistor driven as the complement of the
  main switch conducts in both directions, so the inductor current simply
  goes negative at light load and the converter stays in CCM <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />.
- **"Light load" is relative.** Whether a given load is light depends on
  $L$, $T_s$ and $D$ as well as on $R$; compare $K$ with $K_\mathrm{crit}$
  rather than trusting intuition.
- **Do not use CCM formulas in DCM.** Ripple, peak current and conversion
  ratio all follow different expressions once the current reaches zero.
