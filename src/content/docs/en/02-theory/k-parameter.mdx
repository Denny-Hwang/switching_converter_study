## Intent

- Compute $K$ for a converter and $K_\mathrm{crit}(D)$ for its topology.
- Decide the operating mode, and find the inductance that keeps a converter in CCM down to a given load.
- Identify the operating point (input voltage and load) that is the worst case for the mode boundary.

## Theory

### The boundary condition

A converter with a diode leaves CCM when the inductor current ripple reaches
the dc inductor current: at the boundary the half-ripple $\Delta i_L$ equals
the dc current <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />. When both sides
are written in terms of circuit values, the inductance, the load resistance
and the switching period collect into one dimensionless parameter,

<Eq id="K.def" />

and the boundary becomes a function of the duty ratio alone, the critical
value $K_\mathrm{crit}(D)$. The converter operates in CCM for
$K > K_\mathrm{crit}$ and in DCM for $K < K_\mathrm{crit}$
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />.

$K$ compares the time constant $L/R$ with the switching period. A larger
inductance, a heavier load (smaller $R$) or a higher switching frequency all
raise $K$ and push the converter towards CCM.

For the flyback, $K$ is formed with the magnetizing inductance referred to
the primary and with the actual load resistance; the turns ratio then
appears in the critical value:

<Eq id="Kcrit.flyback" />

The shapes differ. The buck's value is largest at small $D$, approaching 1.
The boost's value is zero at both ends of the duty-ratio range; setting its
derivative to zero shows that it peaks at $D = 1/3$, where it is $4/27$.
The buck-boost's value falls from 1 at small $D$ to zero at large $D$.

### Designing for CCM: the critical inductance

Setting $K$ equal to $K_\mathrm{crit}$ and solving the definition of $K$ for
$L$ gives the smallest inductance that keeps the converter in CCM at a given
load:

<Eq id="L.crit" />

Because $K_\mathrm{crit}$ depends on $D$, and $D$ on the input voltage, the
worst case is the lightest load (largest $R$) at the duty ratio that gives
the largest $K_\mathrm{crit}$ over the input-voltage range. For a buck with a
regulated output this is the highest input voltage, where $D$ is smallest.

## Bench exercise

On a converter with a diode rectifier and an adjustable resistive load,
watch the inductor current with a current probe and increase the load
resistance until the current just touches zero at the end of each period.
Measure the duty ratio and the load resistance at that point, compute $K$
from the inductance and the switching period, and compare it with
$K_\mathrm{crit}(D)$ for the topology.

## Gotchas

- **Use consistent quantities in $K$.** For isolated converters, $K$ uses
  the magnetizing inductance referred to the primary with the actual load
  resistance on the secondary side; the turns ratio then sits in
  $K_\mathrm{crit}$. Mixing referred and unreferred values gives the wrong
  mode <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />.
- **Check the whole input-voltage range.** In closed loop the duty ratio moves
  with the input voltage, and with it $K_\mathrm{crit}$.
- **Compare with the smallest inductance the inductor will have in
  operation**, not with its nominal value, since the critical inductance is a
  minimum.
