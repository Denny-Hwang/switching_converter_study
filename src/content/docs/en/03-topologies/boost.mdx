## Intent

- Find the boost converter's inductor current, current ripple and peak current at an operating point.
- Estimate its output voltage ripple, and explain why it is larger than a buck's.
- State the switch and diode stress, and the two effects that limit a boost at high duty ratio.

## Theory

### Circuit and conversion ratio

<Figure name="boost" />

The boost converter places the inductor in series with the input, a switch
from the inductor's far end to ground, and a diode from that node to the
output capacitor and load <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 2" />. Storing
energy in the inductor while the switch is on and releasing it on top of the
input voltage while the diode conducts raises the output above the input
(<EqRef id="boost.ccm.M" label="boost conversion ratio" />).

While the switch is on, the inductor sees the input voltage and its current
rises linearly <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 2" />:

The switch and the diode carry the inductor current in turn, so both see its
peak value (<EqRef id="ripple.Ipk" label="peak current" />).

The diode current reaches the output in pulses. While the switch is on, the
capacitor alone supplies the load, and its voltage falls

Because the capacitor supplies the full load current for part of every
period, instead of absorbing only a small triangular ripple, the boost needs
more output capacitance than a buck for the same ripple. The derivation is
on the [derivations page](../../02-theory/derivations/#deriv-topologies).

### Stress and limits

With the switch off, the switch blocks the output voltage; with the switch
on, the diode blocks it <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />:

<Eq id="boost.Vds" />

Two effects limit the boost at high duty ratio. The inductor's winding
resistance makes the ratio peak and then collapse
(<EqRef id="boost.ccm.M_RL" label="ratio with winding resistance" />), and the
control-to-output transfer function has a
[right-half-plane zero](../../02-theory/rhp-zero/) that moves down as $D$
rises (<EqRef id="boost.ss.wz" label="RHP zero" />).

## Bench exercise

On a low-power boost evaluation board supplied from a current-limited bench
supply, measure the input current with a current probe: it is the inductor
current, continuous and triangular. Then measure the diode current, or the
output capacitor current, and see the pulses. Compare the measured output
ripple with the prediction at the measured duty ratio and load, then halve
the load resistance and check that the ripple doubles.

## Gotchas

- **The switch cannot disconnect the load.** Even with the switch held off,
  the inductor and the diode connect the input to the output, so the output
  follows the input and the capacitor charges through them at power-up.
- **Low input voltage means high current.** At a fixed output voltage and
  power, halving the input voltage doubles both the step-up ratio and the
  input current; the inductor, the switch and the diode must be rated for
  that current plus the half-ripple.
- **Past the peak of the lossy ratio, more duty ratio gives less output.**
  See the [averaged-models page](../../02-theory/averaged-models/).
