## Intent

- Compute the flyback's conversion ratio in CCM and DCM with the 1:n turns-ratio convention.
- Find the switch and diode stress, and the energy the leakage inductance leaves behind at every turn-off.
- Explain why a fixed duty ratio into a fixed output pins the input voltage of a CCM flyback.

## Theory

### From buck-boost to flyback

Replace the buck-boost's inductor by two coupled windings on one core, with
turns ratio 1:n, where $n$ is the secondary turns divided by the primary
turns, and the result is the flyback converter.
The magnetizing inductance $L_M$, referred to the primary, stores energy
while the switch is on; the secondary winding and the diode deliver it to
the output while the switch is off. The windings isolate the output and
the turns ratio scales the conversion ratio
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />:

<Eq id="flyback.ccm.M" />

$K$ is formed with $L_M$ and the actual load resistance, and the turns
ratio then appears in the critical value
(<EqRef id="Kcrit.flyback" label="flyback critical K" />). In DCM the ratio no
longer depends on the turns ratio <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />:

<Eq id="flyback.dcm.M" />

### Switch and diode stress

While the switch is off, the secondary is clamped to the output plus the
diode drop, and that voltage appears on the primary divided by $n$. The
switch therefore blocks the input voltage plus this reflected voltage
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />:

<Eq id="flyback.Vds_off" />

While the switch is on, the secondary sees $n V_g$, and the diode blocks it
on top of the output voltage:

<Eq id="flyback.diode.VR" />

A smaller $n$ lowers the diode stress but raises the reflected voltage on
the switch; the turns ratio trades one stress for the other.

### DCM peak current and the loss-free resistor

In DCM the magnetizing current starts from zero every period, so its peak
depends only on the input voltage, the on-time and $L_M$
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />:

<Eq id="flyback.Ipk.dcm" />

The energy stored per period sets the input power, and the input behaves as
a loss-free resistor (<EqRef id="dcm.P_in" label="DCM input power" />,
<EqRef id="lfr.R_in" label="input resistance" />).

### Leakage inductance

Not all of the primary flux links the secondary. The leakage inductance
carries the primary current at turn-off, and its energy cannot reach the
output <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 4" />:

<Eq id="flyback.leak.E" />

A clamp or snubber must absorb this energy in every period, which sets a
lower bound on its dissipation:

<Eq id="flyback.leak.P" />

### Fixed duty ratio into a fixed output

If the output is held by a battery or a regulated bus, and the duty ratio
is fixed, CCM volt-second balance leaves only one input voltage that fits:

<Eq id="flyback.V_crit" />

Below it the flyback runs in DCM and draws power like a resistor. When a
current-limited source drives the input, its voltage rises until it reaches
this value, where CCM begins and holds the input there: the converter then
acts as a constant-voltage sink. At that point the switch blocks

## Bench exercise

On a low-power flyback evaluation board, watch the switch voltage while the
switch is off. You should see a short spike at turn-off (the leakage energy
being clamped), then a plateau at the input voltage plus the reflected
output voltage, and, in DCM, ringing once the secondary current has reached
zero. Measure the plateau and compare it with the switch-stress equation at
the measured input and output voltages.

## Gotchas

- **The leakage energy has to go somewhere.** Without a clamp or snubber the
  turn-off spike rises until something breaks down; with one, the clamp
  dissipates at least the leakage power above
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 4" />.
- **An open-loop flyback must not lose its load.** In DCM the output rises as
  the load is removed (<EqRef id="flyback.dcm.M" label="DCM ratio" /> grows
  as $K$ falls), and with it the reflected voltage, until the clamp limits the
  switch voltage.
- **A fixed-duty-ratio flyback from a current-limited source pins its input.**
  Once CCM begins, the input sits at the critical voltage above instead of
  rising further.
