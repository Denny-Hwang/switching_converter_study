## Intent

- Compute the forward converter's conversion ratio and output inductor ripple.
- Explain why the transformer must be reset in every period, and how the reset winding limits the duty ratio.
- Find the switch stress set by the reset winding, and the trade-off it implies.

## Theory

### A transformer-isolated buck

In the forward converter the transformer passes energy directly while the
switch is on: the secondary applies $n V_g$ to a buck-type output stage (a
diode, a freewheeling diode and an L-C filter). Unlike the flyback's
coupled inductor, the transformer is not meant to store energy
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />. The ratio is the buck's, scaled by
the turns ratio:

### Transformer reset

A real transformer has a finite magnetizing inductance, so the
magnetizing current rises while the switch is on. It must return to zero
before the next period, or it grows from period to period until the core
saturates <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />. A reset winding, with
$n_r$ its turns divided by the primary turns, returns the magnetizing energy
to the input while the switch is off. Volt-second balance on the magnetizing inductance
then limits the duty ratio:

<Eq id="forward.reset.Dmax" />

During the reset the reset winding clamps the primary at the input voltage
scaled by $1/n_r$, with reversed polarity, and the switch blocks

<Eq id="forward.Vds" />

More reset turns (larger $n_r$) lower the switch stress but also lower the
maximum duty ratio, and fewer turns do the opposite. With $n_r = 1$ the duty
ratio is limited to one half and the switch blocks twice the input voltage.

## Bench exercise

On a single-switch forward evaluation board, watch the switch voltage.
While the switch is off, you should see a plateau at the reset voltage for
as long as the magnetizing current takes to return to zero, followed by
ringing that settles at the input voltage. Measure the plateau duration at
two duty ratios and check that the reset time grows in proportion to the
on-time.

## Gotchas

- **Exceeding the reset limit saturates the core.** If the duty ratio is
  pushed past the limit, even during a transient, the magnetizing current
  does not return to zero and grows from period to period
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />; a duty-ratio clamp in the
  controller prevents this.
- **The reset winding sets the switch rating.** Choosing $n_r$ fixes both the
  maximum duty ratio and the switch stress.
- **Output stage equals buck.** Output ripple, peak current and the CCM/DCM
  boundary of the output inductor follow the [buck page](../buck/), with
  $n V_g$ in place of $V_g$.
