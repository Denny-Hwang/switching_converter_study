## Intent

- Turn a filter inductor's specification (inductance, peak current, flux limit, copper-loss budget) into a core size with the core geometrical constant $K_g$.
- Carry the chosen core through the turns, the gap and the wire, and check each constraint.
- Recognize when core loss rather than saturation limits the flux density.

## Theory

### Four constraints

A filter inductor must meet four constraints at once
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 11" />:

1. **Flux density.** At the peak current the flux density stays below
   $B_\mathrm{max}$, which sets the fewest turns
   (<EqRef id="mag.N_Bmax" label="turns for a flux limit" />).
2. **Inductance.** With those turns, the gap sets the inductance
   (<EqRef id="mag.gap_length" label="gap length" />).
3. **Window.** The copper fills no more of the window than can be wound, the
   window utilization $K_u$ (<EqRef id="wind.fill" label="window utilization" />).
4. **Resistance.** The winding's resistance stays within the copper-loss
   budget $R_\mathrm{dc}$ (<EqRef id="wind.dcr" label="dc resistance" />).

The turns, the gap and the wire can each meet one constraint; the core must
leave room to meet them together.

### The core geometrical constant

constraints leaves the specification on one side and the core's dimensions
on the other <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 11" />:

The core's dimensions combine into the same quantity

A core whose own $K_g$ is at least the required value can meet the flux
limit, the window and the resistance budget together; the gap then sets the
inductance. The product $L I_\mathrm{pk}$ and the flux limit enter squared,
the resistance budget and $K_u$ once: a higher flux limit shrinks the core
most. The gap does not enter $K_g$: it is chosen after the core.

### The procedure

With a core of large enough $K_g$ <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 11" />:

1. **Turns.** The fewest turns that keep the flux density at $B_\mathrm{max}$
   (<EqRef id="mag.N_Bmax" label="turns for a flux limit" />), rounded up to a
   whole turn. The magnetics designer takes them at the core's smallest
   cross-section, where the flux density is highest.
2. **Gap.** The gap that gives the inductance with those turns
   (<EqRef id="mag.gap_length" label="gap length" />). Fringing flux adds
   to the gap's inductance, so a real gap must be somewhat longer
   <Cite key="dixon2001" />; for a table core, the data sheet's gapped sets
   give measured values.
3. **Wire.** The largest copper area per turn that fits: the window times
   $K_u$, shared by the turns (<EqRef id="wind.fill" label="window utilization" />).
4. **Check.** The winding's resistance
   (<EqRef id="wind.dcr" label="dc resistance" />) then meets the budget,
   provided the core's $K_g$ exceeds the requirement by enough to cover the
   turns added by rounding up.

### When core loss limits the flux density

The procedure limits the flux density at the peak current. In a filter
inductor with a small ripple the flux is mostly dc, which causes no core
loss, so saturation sets the limit <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
With a large ripple, as in a DCM inductor or a flyback transformer, the ac
flux amplitude (<EqRef id="mag.B_ac" label="ac flux amplitude" />) causes core
loss (<EqRef id="loss.steinmetz" label="Steinmetz equation" />) that can
limit the flux before saturation does; the design must then also keep the
core loss within its budget <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 12" />.

## Bench exercise

Wind the design on a gapped core set. Measure its inductance with an LCR
meter at a frequency well below its self-resonance, and its dc resistance
with four wires (see [Measuring magnetics](../measurement/)). Then find the
current at which it saturates with the pulse test described there, and
compare it with the design's peak current.

## Gotchas

- **$K_g$ leaves out core loss.** It covers the flux limit, the window and
  the copper loss. With a large flux swing, check the core loss as well
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 12" />.
- **Round up, then check again.** Rounding the turns up, or taking them at a
  smaller cross-section, adds turns and resistance: a core with barely the
  required $K_g$ can then miss the budget.
- **The gap estimate ignores fringing.** Use a data sheet's gapped set, or
  expect a real gap somewhat longer than the estimate
  <Cite key="dixon2001" />.
