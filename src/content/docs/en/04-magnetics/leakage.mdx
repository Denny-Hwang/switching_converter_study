## Intent

- Explain leakage inductance as the flux that links one winding but not the other, stored mostly between the windings.
- Relate the coupling coefficient, the short-circuit inductance and the leakage inductances of the transformer model.
- Estimate the leakage of a winding arrangement, and what it costs in a flyback.

## Theory

### Coupled windings

Two windings on one core share most of their flux, but not all of it. At
their terminals they behave as two self-inductances, $L_{11}$ and $L_{22}$,
and a mutual inductance $L_{12}$ <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />,
<Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 13" />. How much of the flux they share
is the coupling coefficient:

### The short-circuit test

Shorting the secondary holds its voltage, the rate of change of its flux
linkage, at zero: its current then cancels most of the flux the two windings
share, and the primary sees mainly the two windings' leakage (derived here):

<Eq id="xfmr.L_sc" />

With $k$ close to 1 this is a small fraction of $L_{11}$: an impedance meter
at the primary, with the secondary shorted, measures it directly.

### The transformer model

The transformer model splits each winding's inductance into a leakage
inductance and the magnetizing inductance $L_M$ the two share, with an ideal
1:$n$ transformer between them <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />.
Seen from the primary with the secondary shorted:

<Eq id="xfmr.L_sc_T" />

Since $L_M$ is much larger than the leakage inductances, the short-circuit
test measures the sum of the two leakage inductances referred to the
primary, the leakage $L_\mathrm{lk}$ that a flyback's clamp has to absorb.
It does not tell how that sum divides between the windings.

### Where the leakage flux lives

The leakage flux runs through the winding window. Its energy follows from the
magnetomotive force across the window: it rises through the primary's
layers, stays at its peak across the spacing, and falls back to zero
through the secondary (<EqRef id="xfmr.leakage.ps" label="leakage, P-S" />)
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. Splitting the primary around the
secondary halves the peak and brings the leakage down to about a quarter
(<EqRef id="xfmr.leakage.psp" label="leakage, P-S-P" />). Only the winding
geometry and the permeability of free space enter these equations: the core
material and the gap set the magnetizing inductance, not the leakage.

### What leakage costs

When a flyback's switch turns off, the energy stored in the leakage
inductance (<EqRef id="flyback.leak.E" label="leakage energy" />) cannot
reach the secondary. It goes to a clamp or a snubber in every period
(<EqRef id="flyback.leak.P" label="leakage power" />)
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 4" />; see
[snubbers and clamps](../snubbers-clamps/).

## Bench exercise

Measure a transformer's primary inductance with the secondary open, then
with it shorted by a short, wide link, with an LCR meter reading the series
inductance. Choose a test frequency well below the self-resonance and high
enough that the shorted reading no longer changes with frequency (see
[Measuring magnetics](../measurement/#leakage-coupling-and-turns-ratio)). The
ratio of the two readings gives $k$ through the short-circuit equation. Then
connect the two windings in series, aiding and opposing: the difference of
the two readings is four times the mutual inductance
<Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 13" />. Compare the measured leakage
with the estimate from the winding geometry.

## Gotchas

- **The short adds its own inductance.** A long or thin shorting wire adds
  to the reading; use a short, wide link.
- **Too low a test frequency reads high.** With the secondary shorted, the
  magnetizing inductance's reactance must be much larger than the
  secondary's resistance referred to the primary; otherwise it carries part
  of the current and the reading rises towards the open-circuit inductance.
- **The test gives the sum.** The short-circuit test measures the primary's
  and the secondary's leakage together, referred to the primary.
- **A gap does not remove leakage.** Gapping the core lowers $L_M$ and $k$,
  but the leakage inductance itself is set by the windings.
