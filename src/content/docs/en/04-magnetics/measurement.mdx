## Intent

- Measure a wound part's inductance, leakage, turns ratio and dc resistance, and know what limits each reading.
- Find the current at which a core saturates with a voltage pulse.
- Recognize readings distorted by self-resonance, by the test leads, and by the missing dc current.

## Theory

### What an impedance meter reads

An LCR meter applies a small sinusoidal signal at its test frequency and
reports the part's effective value there, its parasitics included
<Cite key="keysight_5950_3000" />. A winding's turns have capacitance
between them, in parallel with its inductance
(<EqRef id="passive.f_srf" label="self-resonant frequency" />), and the
reading rises as the test frequency approaches the self-resonance:

<Eq id="meas.L_app" />

A test frequency well below the self-resonance reads the inductance; at a
tenth of it the reading is 1 % high.

### DC resistance

A two-wire ohmmeter reads the winding together with its test leads and
their contacts. The four-wire (Kelvin) method passes the current through one
pair of leads and senses the voltage with another, so the leads' resistance
stays out of the reading <Cite key="keithley_llmh7" />. A winding of a few
turns can have a resistance well below that of the leads.

### Leakage, coupling and turns ratio

With the other winding shorted, a winding's inductance is its leakage
(<EqRef id="xfmr.L_sc" label="short-circuit inductance" />); with it open,
its full inductance (see [leakage inductance](../leakage/)). The ratio of
the open-circuit voltages at a low test frequency gives the turns ratio
(<EqRef id="xfmr.V2" label="ideal transformer" />).

### Saturation

An impedance meter's small signal and zero dc current do not show
saturation. A voltage step across the winding makes its current ramp at the
voltage over the inductance (<EqRef id="ind.di" label="inductor current" />).
Past the saturation flux density the core's permeability collapses and the
inductance falls sharply <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" /> (see
[magnetic materials and the B-H curve](../../01-physics/ferrites-bh/)), so the ramp
steepens: the current where the slope starts to rise is the saturation
current.

### The B-H loop

With two windings, Ampère's law gives the field intensity from the
primary's current (<EqRef id="mag.H_ampere" label="Ampère's law" />), and
Faraday's law the flux density from the integral of the open secondary's
voltage (<EqRef id="mag.dB_faraday" label="Faraday's law" />)
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. Plotted against each other, the
two trace the core's B-H loop.

## Bench exercise

Measure a winding with many turns, such as a flyback primary, at each test
frequency of an LCR meter, and find its self-resonance with an impedance
analyzer or a frequency sweep. Estimate its capacitance from the
self-resonance (<EqRef id="passive.f_srf" label="self-resonant frequency" />),
and compare the readings with the apparent-inductance equation. Then measure
its dc resistance with two wires and with four: the difference is the
leads and their contacts.

## Gotchas

- **Test frequency against self-resonance.** A winding with many turns
  resonates low. The meter reads the effective value at its test frequency,
  parasitics included <Cite key="keysight_5950_3000" />: close to the
  self-resonance, that is well above the inductance
  (<EqRef id="meas.L_app" label="apparent inductance" />).
- **Two-wire resistance includes the leads.** Use four wires for a winding's
  dc resistance <Cite key="keithley_llmh7" />.
- **No dc current, no saturation.** A meter's reading holds at the peak
  current only if the core does not saturate there: check that with a
  current pulse at the design's peak current.
