## Intent

- Tell a clamp, which limits the peak voltage and absorbs the leakage energy, from a snubber, which damps the ringing.
- Find a ringing node's parasitic capacitance and inductance from two measurements, and size an RC snubber from them.
- Estimate what a clamp and a snubber dissipate.

## Theory

### Clamps

When a flyback's switch turns off, its leakage inductance keeps the current
flowing and drives the drain above the input voltage plus the reflected
output voltage (<EqRef id="flyback.V_OR" label="reflected voltage" />) until
a clamp limits it <Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />
(<EqRef id="clamp.Vds" label="clamped switch voltage" />). The clamp absorbs
more than the leakage energy, and the closer its voltage is to the reflected
voltage, the longer the leakage current takes to reset
(<EqRef id="clamp.t_reset" label="reset time" />) and the more it dissipates
(<EqRef id="clamp.P" label="clamp power" />) <Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />. An
RCD clamp's voltage settles where its resistor dissipates the clamp's power
(<EqRef id="clamp.rcd.V" label="RCD clamp voltage" />). A TVS clamps at its
breakdown voltage plus its dynamic resistance times the current
(<EqRef id="tvs.V_clamp" label="TVS clamping voltage" />)
<Cite key="st_an316" />. The [clamp check](../../design/clamp-check/) sizes
both.

### The RC snubber

A resistor across the node, in series with a capacitor, damps the ring. Its
value is the ring's characteristic impedance <Cite key="nexperia_an11160" />:

<Eq id="snub.R" />

The snubber capacitor must pass the ringing to the resistor, so it has to be
larger than $C_\mathrm{par}$. With it at three times $C_\mathrm{par}$ or more,
the ring's damping ratio is about 0.5 and it falls to a few per cent of its
amplitude within one period; with it equal to $C_\mathrm{par}$, the damping
ratio is only 0.16. Every edge charges or discharges the snubber capacitor
through the resistor, which dissipates the same energy whatever its value:

<Eq id="snub.P" />

A larger capacitor damps better and dissipates more: a snubber is a
compromise between damping and loss <Cite key="nexperia_an11160" />.

## Bench exercise

Probe the switch node with a short ground spring and read the ringing
frequency after the switching edge. Add a C0G capacitor of a few times the
expected parasitic capacitance, close to the device, and read the frequency
again. Compute $C_\mathrm{par}$, $L_\mathrm{par}$ and the snubber resistor;
build the snubber with a resistor rated for its dissipation, and check the
damping and the resistor's temperature.

## Gotchas

- **Judge a TVS by its clamping voltage at the real current.** Its clamping
  voltage is its breakdown voltage plus its dynamic resistance times the
  current, not the breakdown voltage alone <Cite key="st_an316" />.
- **A clamp voltage close to the reflected voltage wastes power.** The clamp
  then takes much more than the leakage energy <Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />.
- **The snubber's loss does not depend on its resistor.** It is set by the
  capacitor, the voltage swing and the switching frequency.
