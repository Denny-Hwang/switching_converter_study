## Intent

- Open and run the SPICE library's LTspice schematics, and plot what their READMEs list.
- Work out how long a simulation from rest must run before its last periods show the steady state.
- Know which directives the schematics carry, and why.

## Theory

### The library in LTspice

The repository's `sim/ltspice/` folder has the five basic converters as
LTspice schematics <Cite key="ltspice" />, one folder per converter with a
README that says what to plot and what to expect. Each schematic is the same
circuit as the ngspice netlist of the same name in `sim/ngspice/`: the
library's script reads every schematic back into a netlist and compares it
part by part and node by node, and the nets carry the netlist's node names.
Open a schematic and press Run: its directives come with it.

- `.param` sets the values of the synthetic example that the simulator's
  preset of the same name uses.
- `.model` gives the near-ideal switch and diode.
- `.tran` runs from rest, with every capacitor empty and every inductor
  without current, for several hundred periods, with a time step no longer
  than a two-hundredth of a period.
- `.options method=gear reltol=1e-4` chooses the integration method and the
  tolerance. The [ngspice page](../ngspice/) explains why.

The ngspice netlists also carry `.meas` statements that average each quantity
and find its extremes over the last period. In LTspice, zoom in on the last
period, or add the same statements to the schematic.

### How long to run

A run from rest has to pass through the whole start-up before its last period
shows the steady state. In CCM, the output filter and the load ring after the
start, and the ring's envelope decays with a time constant set by the load
and the capacitance alone:

<Eq id="filter.tau_env" />

The error left after the start-up shrinks by the same factor in every
envelope time constant. To bring it down to a fraction of what it was at
the start takes:

<Eq id="sim.periods_settle" />

A light load rings longer, and each factor of ten in accuracy adds the same
number of periods. The library's script checks each run at the end: the
output's average over the last period must lie within one part in ten
thousand of its average over the period before, or the case fails as not
settled. In DCM the inductor current returns to zero in every period, so the
inductor carries nothing from one period to the next: the filter no longer
rings as an L-C pair, and the output settles in another way
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 15" />.
The library's DCM cases run longer than its CCM ones.

### Integration settings

With LTspice's default settings the library's DCM flyback shows dips of the
drain voltage far below zero that the circuit does not have. The
`.options` line removes them, as it does in ngspice; the library records a
one-time check with LTspice 26.1.1, in which LTspice's own netlists matched
the library's and its results agreed with ngspice's within 0.3 % of each
quantity's largest magnitude. The [ngspice page](../ngspice/) shows what
decides this: the factor by which each step multiplies a fast mode
(<EqRef id="num.gain_tr" label="the trapezoidal rule's factor" />).

## Bench exercise

Start a buck at a fixed duty ratio into a resistor and capture its output
from the moment it starts switching. Measure the envelope's decay from two
peaks of the ring a known time apart, and compare the time constant with
twice the load resistance times the output capacitance. Count the periods
until the output stays within one percent of its final value, and compare
with the formula for one percent left. Then run the library's schematic with
your values and the same start.

## Gotchas

- **A measurement over a period that has not settled** measures the start-up.
  Check the output's average over the last two periods, as the library does.
- **The envelope formula holds in CCM, with a ringing filter.** In DCM, or
  with a filter damped so heavily that it does not ring, the output settles
  another way (<EqRef id="filter.tau_env" label="envelope time constant" />).
- **A spike that the circuit cannot make** is often numerical: check the
  `.options` line before you look for a cause in the circuit.
