## Intent

- Read the Miller plateau and its charge from a switch's gate-charge curve.
- Estimate the gate current and the time the drain voltage takes to switch from the driver's voltage and the gate loop's resistance.
- Size a bootstrap capacitor for a high-side driver.

## Theory

### The Miller plateau

A MOSFET's gate charges through its gate loop: the driver's output, an
external resistor and the device's internal gate resistance, together
$R_G$. At turn-on there is a stretch where the gate does not rise: while
the drain voltage falls, the gate-source voltage stays constant, on the
Miller plateau, and all the gate current available from the driver is
diverted to discharge the gate-drain capacitance <Cite key="balogh2018" />.
The charge the gate takes meanwhile is the gate-drain charge
$Q_\mathrm{GD}$ of the data sheet's gate-charge curve
<Cite key="vishay_an608a" />. The gate itself sits at the plateau voltage
$V_\mathrm{pl}$, so the driver's on voltage $V_\mathrm{GS}$ (the drive
voltage, as in the gate-drive loss) less the plateau drives the gate
current:

<Eq id="gate.I_on" />

At turn-off the driver pulls the gate to zero. On the plateau the drain
voltage now rises, and the gate current is the current that charges the
gate-drain capacitance <Cite key="balogh2018" where="MOSFET turn-off" />;
the plateau voltage itself drives it:

<Eq id="gate.I_off" />

The plateau lasts as long as the gate current takes to deliver
$Q_\mathrm{GD}$, and the drain voltage makes its transition meanwhile:

<Eq id="gate.t_pl" />

With the same gate-loop resistance at both edges, a plateau below half the
drive voltage leaves less current at turn-off than at turn-on, so the switch
turns off more slowly than it turns on; a smaller resistance at turn-off
(a driver that sinks more strongly than it sources) raises the turn-off
current in the same equation. Each cycle the driver delivers the whole gate
charge, and the gate loop's resistances (the driver's output, the external
resistor and the internal gate resistance) dissipate the gate-drive power
between them (<EqRef id="loss.gate" label="gate-drive loss" />).

### The bootstrap capacitor

A high-side switch's gate is driven from a supply that floats on the switch
node. A bootstrap capacitor, charged while the switch node is low, is that
supply <Cite key="ti_slua887" />: while the high side is on, it alone
supplies the gate charge and whatever the driver draws, and its voltage
droops by that charge over its capacitance. For an allowed droop:

<Eq id="boot.C" />

A capacitor below the minimum value can let the driver's undervoltage
lockout turn the high-side switch off <Cite key="ti_slua887" />.

### GaN switches

The maximum gate-source voltage of an eGaN FET (EPC's enhancement-mode GaN
switch) is low compared with silicon MOSFETs <Cite key="epc_wp008" />. The
gate loop is a loop too: its inductance develops a voltage over every edge
of the gate current (<EqRef id="layout.v_spike" label="loop voltage" />),
and it rings with the gate's capacitance, so the gate's voltage can
overshoot the drive voltage toward that lower limit.

## Bench exercise

With a voltage probe on the gate and another on the drain of a low-side
switch, both referred to its source at circuit ground, find the plateau
during turn-on: the gate's rise stops while the drain voltage falls. A
switch whose source is not at ground needs differential probes: a probe's
ground lead is tied to the oscilloscope's earth. Measure the plateau's
voltage and length. The probe sees the gate pin, outside the internal gate
resistance, so the plateau it shows sits above the die's at turn-on and
below it at turn-off, by the gate current times that resistance. Compare
the plateau's length with the gate-drain charge over the gate current the
equations give; the data sheet gives that charge at its own test drain
voltage, and a larger swing takes more. Repeat at turn-off, and with a
larger gate resistor.

## Gotchas

- **A plateau close to the drive voltage** leaves little voltage across the
  gate loop, so little current and a slow transition
  (<EqRef id="gate.I_on" label="gate current at turn-on" />).
- **Turn-off slower than turn-on:** with the same gate-loop resistance at
  both edges and the plateau below half the drive voltage, the gate's
  current at turn-off is the smaller of the two
  (<EqRef id="gate.I_off" label="gate current at turn-off" />).
- **A bootstrap capacitor that is too small** droops until the driver's
  undervoltage lockout turns the high side off <Cite key="ti_slua887" />.
- **An eGaN gate's lower voltage limit** <Cite key="epc_wp008" /> leaves
  less room for the ringing the gate loop's own inductance adds.
