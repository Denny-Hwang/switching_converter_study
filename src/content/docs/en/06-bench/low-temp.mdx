## Intent

- Estimate the ripple an aluminum electrolytic capacitor's ESR develops in the cold.
- Know what a ceramic capacitor's temperature code does not tell.
- Read a battery's terminal voltage in the Rint model, and know why a lithium-ion cell is not charged in the cold.

## Theory

### Aluminum electrolytic capacitors

An aluminum electrolytic capacitor's ESR can increase more than tenfold at
its low-temperature limit <Cite key="cde_ae_guide" />; the part's data sheet
gives its own low-temperature figures. The ripple voltage across the ESR
rises in proportion
(<EqRef id="cap.esr.ripple" label="ripple voltage across the ESR" />): a
converter whose output ripple meets its limit at room temperature can
exceed it in the cold.

### Ceramic capacitors

A class-2 ceramic capacitor's code, such as X7R, describes how its
capacitance changes with temperature, and implies nothing about voltage
coefficients <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />: what the dc voltage across it
takes away is not in the code. The dc voltage alone can leave a small
fraction of the nominal capacitance: the article's own example, a 4.7 µF Y5V capacitor rated 6.3 V, keeps 0.33 µF at 5 V at room temperature <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />,
and temperature changes it again. Check the capacitance at the dc voltage it
runs at, across the temperatures it will see.

### Batteries

In the Rint model a battery is its open-circuit voltage behind an internal
resistance, and both are functions of the state of charge, the state of
health and the temperature <Cite key="he2011" where="Sec. 2.1" />. A charging
current lifts the terminals above the open-circuit voltage:

<Eq id="bat.v_term" />

A charger that holds the terminals at a voltage limit therefore delivers
less current into a battery whose resistance is higher: take the resistance
at the temperature the battery runs at.

At low temperature, a lithium-ion cell's carbon anode receives lithium ions
less readily, and metallic lithium deposits form more easily on its negative
electrode. Below the lower charging temperature specified for the cell, it
must not be charged <Cite key="jeita_liion" />.

## Bench exercise

Measure a converter's output ripple at room temperature, then with its
output capacitor cooled (a cold chamber, or cold spray on the capacitor
alone), with the probe's ground connection kept short
([probes](../probes/)). Where the ESR's part dominates the ripple, divide
the ripple by the peak-to-peak ripple current into the capacitor to
estimate the ESR, and compare the two temperatures. Watch for condensation as the capacitor warms again. For a
battery, log its terminal voltage and current at two temperatures while it
discharges at a constant current, and estimate its resistance from a step
in the current at each.

## Gotchas

- **An electrolytic's ESR** can increase more than tenfold at its
  low-temperature limit <Cite key="cde_ae_guide" />.
- **A ceramic's code says nothing about its dc voltage**
  <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />.
- **A lithium-ion cell below its lower charging temperature** must not be
  charged <Cite key="jeita_liion" />.
- **A charger at its voltage limit** delivers less current into a battery
  of higher resistance (<EqRef id="bat.v_term" label="terminal voltage" />).
