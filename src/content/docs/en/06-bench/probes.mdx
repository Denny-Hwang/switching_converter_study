## Intent

- Know what a probe's ground lead adds to a measurement of a fast edge.
- Relate a measurement system's bandwidth to the fastest rise it can show.
- Measure a converter's output ripple with a short ground connection.

## Theory

### The ground lead

A probe measures between its tip and its ground connection, and the leads
that reach them form a loop. That loop introduces parasitic inductance,
which inaccurately increases the amplitude of switching transients
<Cite key="adi_an1144" />. With the probe's input capacitance it forms a
series L-C that every fast edge excites, ringing at its resonant frequency:
a ringing of the probe, not of the circuit.

<Eq id="probe.f_ring" />

A shorter ground makes the loop smaller: the tip-and-barrel method removes
the long ground lead and connects the probe's barrel to the ground
reference directly <Cite key="adi_an1144" />.

### Bandwidth and rise time

A probe and an oscilloscope together respond to a step no faster than their
bandwidth allows. For a single-pole response, the 10-90 % rise time follows
from the bandwidth:

<Eq id="probe.t_rise" />

Historically, oscilloscopes' responses tended to follow this rule
approximately <Cite key="tek_bw_risetime" />; the constant depends on the shape of the
response, and a flat-response oscilloscope measures rise times more
accurately than a Gaussian-response one of the same bandwidth
<Cite key="keysight_5988_8008" />. An ideal step, infinitely fast, is shown
rising in the system's own rise time: to see an edge's own rise time, the
system's must be well below it.

### Output ripple

A converter's output ripple is small next to the output voltage, and the
switching transients that ride on it are fast: the loop of a long ground
lead exaggerates them <Cite key="adi_an1144" />, and they look like ripple.
Measure it with the tip-and-barrel method at the output capacitor
<Cite key="adi_an1144" />. A bandwidth limit
on the oscilloscope's channel attenuates what lies above it
(<EqRef id="rc.gain" label="first-order low-pass" />): set it well above the
ripple's own frequencies, or it attenuates the ripple as well.

## Bench exercise

Measure the same switching edge twice: with the probe's long ground lead,
and with a ground spring or the tip-and-barrel method. Compare the overshoot
and the ringing's frequency, and estimate the lead's inductance from that
frequency and the probe's input capacitance on its data sheet. Then measure
the output ripple both ways, with the channel's bandwidth limit on and off.

## Gotchas

- **A ringing that moves with the ground lead** is the probe's, not the
  circuit's: shorten the ground and it changes <Cite key="adi_an1144" />.
- **An edge much faster than the system** is shown with about the system's
  rise time (<EqRef id="probe.t_rise" label="rise time" />).
- **A bandwidth limit near the ripple's frequencies** attenuates the ripple
  itself (<EqRef id="rc.gain" label="first-order low-pass" />).
