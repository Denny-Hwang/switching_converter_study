## Intent

- Size a fixed-duty-ratio DCM flyback so that it presents a source's resistance.
- Find the source amplitude above which it leaves DCM, and know what it does then.
- Know what voltage its switch blocks, and why more power through the same resistance needs more of it.

## Theory

### Sizing the loss-free resistor

A flyback in DCM with a fixed duty ratio presents the
<EqRef id="lfr.R_in" label="loss-free resistor" /> at its input
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 15 (AC and DC Equivalent Circuit Modeling of the Discontinuous Conduction Mode)" />.
Solved for the magnetizing inductance, that resistance gives the inductance
a design needs:

<Eq id="lfr.L_M" />

To match a linear source, the input resistance is set to the source
resistance (<EqRef id="lfr.eta" label="extraction by a loss-free resistor" />).
A lower switching frequency, or a larger duty ratio, needs a larger
inductance for the same resistance. The output voltage does not enter: in
DCM the input resistance depends only on the inductance, the switching
frequency and the duty ratio.

### Where DCM ends

The flyback stays in DCM while its input voltage is below the
<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />, which the
output voltage, the diode's drop, the duty ratio and the turns ratio set. A
source strong enough to raise the divider voltage
(<EqRef id="lfr.Vg" label="bus voltage of a loss-free resistor" />) above it
drives the flyback into CCM. With the output fixed, the CCM conversion ratio
then allows only one input voltage, the critical one
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6 (Converter Circuits)" />: the bus is
held there, and the flyback becomes a constant-voltage sink, whose share of
the available power depends on the source's amplitude
(<EqRef id="src.cv_extraction" label="constant-voltage extraction" />). The
simulator's current-limited source shows the bus settling at the critical
voltage.

### What the switch blocks, and more power

The switch blocks the input voltage plus the output voltage reflected
through the turns ratio (<EqRef id="flyback.Vds_off" label="switch voltage" />),
and the leakage inductance's spike adds on top
([snubbers and clamps](../../04-magnetics/snubbers-clamps/)). Through the
same input resistance, a larger power needs a higher input voltage:

<Eq id="lfr.Vg_power" />

so drawing more power through a matched flyback raises its switch's voltage.
That holds up to the critical voltage. There CCM holds the bus, and while
the flyback switches, the switch voltage stays at <EqRef id="flyback.Vds_clamped" label="its CCM value" />
whatever power the source then pushes in; the largest power the flyback
draws as a resistor is <EqRef id="dcm.P_in" label="the power a DCM converter draws" />
at the critical voltage.

## Bench exercise

Build the flyback with its duty ratio fixed and a bus capacitor across its
input, large enough that with the series resistor it averages the pulsed
input current over many switching periods, and feed it from a bench supply
through a series resistor equal to its computed input resistance. Measure
the input voltage: it should sit at half the supply's voltage. Raise the
supply slowly until the input voltage stops following it; that is the
critical input voltage, to compare with the formula. The supply must reach
twice that voltage, several hundred volts with the example's numbers, and
the bus capacitor holds what it reaches: keep the circuit enclosed, and
discharge the capacitor through a resistor before touching it. A larger
turns ratio n (N_s/N_p) lowers the critical voltage
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />) and keeps
the exercise at a safer voltage. At each step, capture
the switch's drain voltage with a probe rated for it
([probes](../../06-bench/probes/)), and read the plateau before the leakage
spike against the formula.

## Gotchas

- **Above the critical input voltage the flyback no longer matches.**
  CCM holds the bus there (<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />).
- **The switch voltage includes the reflected output,** and the leakage
  spike adds to it (<EqRef id="flyback.Vds_off" label="switch voltage" />).
- **While the flyback does not switch, the bus charges to the open-circuit
  voltage:** at start-up, in an undervoltage lockout or after a fault,
  nothing draws on the source. The switch then blocks that voltage plus the
  reflected output: rate it for that, or clamp the bus
  ([the design case](../synthetic-case/)).
- **More power through the same resistance needs a higher voltage**
  (<EqRef id="lfr.Vg_power" label="input voltage for a given power" />).
- **The match is forgiving.** The share is flat at its top: a small error
  in the inductance moves the input resistance by the same fraction, and the
  share falls by only about a quarter of that fraction squared
  (<EqRef id="lfr.eta" label="extraction by a loss-free resistor" />).
