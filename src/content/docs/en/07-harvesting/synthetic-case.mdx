## Intent

- Follow one harvesting design from the source to the switch.
- Estimate the average power a loss-free resistor takes from a source whose amplitude follows a slow envelope.
- Check the design at the envelope's peak: DCM, and the switch's voltage.

## Theory

### The source

The case's source is linear: an open-circuit voltage behind a large source
resistance, whose amplitude rises and falls along a slow envelope, the same
half sine over and over. At each instant it can give at most
<EqRef id="src.Pmax" label="the maximum power of a linear source" />, a
quantity that follows the square of the envelope.

### The converter

A fixed-duty-ratio flyback in DCM, sized as a loss-free resistor equal to the
source resistance (<EqRef id="lfr.L_M" label="magnetizing inductance of the loss-free resistor" />),
holds its input at half the source's voltage and takes the whole available
power at every instant (<EqRef id="lfr.eta" label="extraction by a loss-free resistor" />),
as long as two things hold. It must stay in DCM at the envelope's peak, where
the bus is highest (<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />).
And the envelope must be slow against the bus's own time constant, the bus
capacitance times the source resistance in parallel with the input
resistance, so that the bus follows the envelope
<Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 7 (First-Order Circuits)" />.

### The average power

Averaged over the envelope, the power is half its value at the peak, since
the square of a sine averages to half its peak's
<Cite key="alexander2017" where="Ch. 11" />:

<Eq id="lfr.P_env" />

The [source matcher](../../design/source-matcher/) simulates such an envelope
cycle by cycle, and shows the bus held at the critical voltage where the
peak would drive the flyback into CCM.

### The switch

At the envelope's peak the switch blocks the highest voltage it will see: the
bus plus the reflected output (<EqRef id="flyback.Vds_off" label="switch voltage" />),
and the leakage spike on top
([snubbers and clamps](../../04-magnetics/snubbers-clamps/)). A switch rated
for the average, or for the envelope's start, fails at its peak.

## Bench exercise

Do not build the case at its full voltage on an open bench. Scale it down:
divide the source voltage by a factor and keep the resistances, which divides
the bus voltage by that factor and the power by its square; the output and
its reflection through the transformer stay as they are. Emulate the
source with a programmable supply stepping through the envelope behind a
resistor equal to the source resistance, and the load with the flyback at
its fixed duty ratio. Log the input voltage and current at each step,
compute the power, and compare its average over the envelope with the
formula, scaled.

## Gotchas

- **An unloaded bus charges to the open-circuit voltage.** Behind a large
  resistance the source's current is small, but a capacitor across it still
  reaches the full voltage, and holds it.
- **The average needs a slow envelope.** An envelope comparable to the bus's
  time constant leaves the bus behind it, and the loss-free resistor's share
  no longer holds at every instant.
- **Check DCM at the peak, not at the average**
  (<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />).
- **Rate the switch for the peak** (<EqRef id="flyback.Vds_off" label="switch voltage" />).
