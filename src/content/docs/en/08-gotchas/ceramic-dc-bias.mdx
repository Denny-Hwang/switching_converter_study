## Symptom

A converter's output ripple is a few times the value the design predicts,
or a filter's corner sits well away from its calculation, although an LCR
meter reads the ceramic capacitor close to its rated value on the bench.

## Why

A class-2 code such as X7R or X5R states how much the capacitance may
change over a temperature range. It implies nothing about the change with
the dc voltage across the capacitor, so the data for the specific part must
be checked <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />. The dc voltage alone can leave a
small fraction of the rated capacitance: the source's own example, a 4.7 µF Y5V capacitor rated 6.3 V, keeps 0.33 µF at 5 V at room temperature <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />.
How much an X7R or X5R part loses depends on the part: in the article's
data a larger case lost less, and within one case size and dielectric the
voltage rating often made no difference <Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />.
Temperature changes the value again. An LCR meter's small test signal with
no dc voltage does not show the loss.

The ripple follows the capacitance that is actually there
(<EqRef id="buck.ripple.v" label="output voltage ripple" />). An example
buck's output capacitor of <Val example="ceramic-bias" name="C" /> would
ripple by <Val example="ceramic-bias" name="Delta_v" /> (half peak-to-peak); if it keeps
<Val example="ceramic-bias" name="C_bias" /> at its working voltage, the
ripple is <Val example="ceramic-bias" name="Delta_v_bias" />.

## How to confirm

Read the capacitance with the working dc voltage applied: an impedance
meter that can add a dc bias does it, or the manufacturer's curve of
capacitance against dc voltage for that exact part number. In the circuit,
compute the capacitance the measured ripple implies and compare it with the
rating.

## Fix

Size the capacitor from its capacitance at the working dc voltage and at
the extreme temperature, taken from the data of the part itself, and
compare candidate parts by those data, not by their codes
<Cite key="adi_ceramic_caps" />. Where that capacitance falls short, add
capacitors in parallel until the remaining capacitance meets the ripple.
