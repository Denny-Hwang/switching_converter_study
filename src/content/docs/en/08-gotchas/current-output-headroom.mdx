## Symptom

A current-sense monitor with a current output reads correctly at small
currents, but above some current its output stops rising, below the full
scale the design computed from its gain. A larger output resistor makes the
reading clip at an even lower current.

## Why

The monitor forces the sense voltage across its input resistor and sends
the resulting current through its output resistor, so its gain is the ratio
of the two (<EqRef id="sense.current_out_monitor" label="current-output monitor" />)
<Cite key="adi_ltc6101" />. Its output can rise only to a largest voltage,
which its data sheet gives; the LTC6101's data sheet gives it as absolute
values at stated supply voltages <Cite key="adi_ltc6101" />. The output
resistor sets the gain and so the current at which the output reaches that
voltage, but not the voltage itself. The full scale is the lower of that
limit and the ADC's full scale.

In the sense chain's example, the monitor can drive its output to
<Val example="sense-current" name="V_OUTmax" />, and the ADC reads up to
<Val example="sense-current" name="V_FS" />. At the largest current,
<Val example="sense-current" name="I_SENSE" />, the output is
<Val example="sense-current" name="V_OUT" />, and
<Val example="sense-current" name="V_OUThi" /> with the pads and the offset
at the sign that raises it. Doubling the output resistor doubles that
highest output, past both limits.

## How to confirm

Raise the current in steps and read the output at the monitor's own pin.
A line that bends flat at a voltage that does not move when the output
resistor changes is the output's limit; compare it with the data sheet's
largest output at your supply voltage.

## Fix

Choose the output resistor so that the output at the largest current, with
the offset's and the pads' contribution at the sign that raises it, stays
below both the monitor's largest output and the ADC's full scale. The
[sense chain](../../design/sense-chain/) tool computes that full-scale
current and flags a reading that can clip.
