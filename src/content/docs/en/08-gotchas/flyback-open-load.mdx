## Symptom

A flyback that runs at a fixed duty ratio, without feedback, drives its
output far above its normal value when the load is disconnected, during a
test or through a loose connector. A part on the output side fails, or the
primary clamp overheats.

## Why

Each switching period stores energy in the magnetizing inductance and
delivers it to the output. Without a load the output capacitor keeps that
energy, and the output voltage rises from period to period. It stops rising
only when the output's voltage, reflected to the primary, reaches the
clamp's voltage: from then on the clamp takes the energy instead
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_ceiling" label="open-load output voltage" />)
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6 (Converter Circuits)" />. The clamp
then dissipates all of the input power. Take a TVS's clamping voltage at
the peak primary current, and at the temperature that power gives it: its
breakdown voltage rises with temperature, and the ceiling with it
<Cite key="st_an316" />. An RCD clamp sets no fixed ceiling. Its voltage
settles where its resistor dissipates the power it takes, the power balance
behind the <EqRef id="clamp.rcd.V" label="RCD clamp voltage" />
<Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />, which in normal operation is the leakage energy
alone. Without a load that power is the whole input power, so the clamp's,
the output's and the switch's voltages all rise far above their loaded
values.

warms.

## How to confirm

Compute the ceiling from the clamp's voltage, the turns ratio and the
diode's drop (<EqRef id="flyback.V_ceiling" label="open-load output voltage" />),
and compare it with the ratings of every part on the output side. Remove the
load on the bench only if every one of those parts is rated above the
ceiling, the switch is rated above the input voltage plus the clamp's
voltage at the TVS's temperature
(<EqRef id="clamp.Vds" label="switch voltage while the clamp conducts" />),
and the primary clamp is a TVS or a Zener rated for the whole input power;
then watch the output rise towards the ceiling. A lower input voltage
lowers the power the clamp takes, but the ceiling only a little, through
the peak current and the TVS's temperature.

## Fix

Keep the output from being left without a load: regulate it with feedback,
keep a minimum load the converter cannot lose, or clamp the output below
the ratings of what it feeds. If the primary clamp must survive an open
load, rate it for the whole input power; an RCD clamp needs a fixed-voltage
clamp beside it, or an input power limited at no load, as the
[clamp check](../../design/clamp-check/) warns.
