## Symptom

A flyback that runs at a fixed duty ratio, without feedback, drives its
output far above its normal value when the load is disconnected, during a
test or through a loose connector. A part on the output side fails, or the
primary clamp overheats.

## Why

Each switching period stores energy in the magnetizing inductance and
delivers it to the output. Without a load the output capacitor keeps that
energy, and the output voltage rises from period to period. It stops rising
only when the output's voltage, reflected to the primary, reaches the
clamp's voltage: from then on the clamp takes the energy instead
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_ceiling" label="open-load output voltage" />)
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6 (Converter Circuits)" />. The clamp
then dissipates all of the input power. An RCD clamp's voltage rises with
the power it takes, so with one the output rises higher still.

## How to confirm

Compute the ceiling from the clamp's voltage, the turns ratio and the
diode's drop (<EqRef id="flyback.V_ceiling" label="open-load output voltage" />),
and compare it with the ratings of every part on the output side. To see it
on the bench, remove the load with the input current limited and reduced,
and watch the output rise towards the ceiling.

## Fix

Keep the output from being left without a load: regulate it with feedback,
keep a minimum load the converter cannot lose, or clamp the output below
the ratings of what it feeds. If the primary clamp must survive an open
load, rate it for the whole input power.
