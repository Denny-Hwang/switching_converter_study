## Symptom

An LCR meter reads a winding with many turns, a flyback's primary for
example, well above the inductance the design expects, and it reads a
different value at each test frequency: the higher the frequency, the higher
the reading.

## Why

The meter reports the part's effective value at its test frequency,
parasitics included <Cite key="keysight_5950_3000" />. The capacitance
between the turns sits in parallel with the inductance and resonates with it
(<EqRef id="passive.f_srf" label="self-resonant frequency" />). Below that
resonance the pair behaves as a larger inductance, and the reading rises
steeply as the test frequency approaches it
(<EqRef id="meas.L_app" label="apparent inductance" />). Many turns bring
much inductance and much capacitance together, so their resonance sits low.

For example, a winding of <Val example="lcr-srf" name="L" /> with
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="C_p" /> across it resonates at
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="f_srf" />. A meter reads it as
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="L_app_lo" sig={4} /> at
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="f_lo" />, and as
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="L_app_hi" /> at
<Val example="lcr-srf" name="f_hi" />.

## How to confirm

Read the winding at two or more of the meter's test frequencies. A reading
that rises with the frequency comes from the resonance; the inductance
itself does not depend on the test frequency. To find the resonance, sweep
the winding's impedance with an impedance analyzer: above the resonance the
part is capacitive (<EqRef id="passive.f_srf" label="self-resonant frequency" />).

## Fix

Measure at a test frequency well below the self-resonance: at a tenth of it
the reading is 1 % high (<EqRef id="meas.L_app" label="apparent inductance" />).
If the meter's test frequencies all sit too close, find the self-resonance
with a sweep and correct the reading with the same equation.
