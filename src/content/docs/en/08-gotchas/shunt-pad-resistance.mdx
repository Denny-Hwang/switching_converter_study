## Symptom

Currents read through a shunt of a few milliohms are high by the same
fraction at every current: a gain error, not an offset. The error is larger
the smaller the shunt.

## Why

With a shunt of a few milliohms or less, the resistance of the solder
becomes a substantial part of the sense element's resistance
<Cite key="osullivan2012" />. If the amplifier's sense connections leave
from the far side of the pads, the amplifier sees the current through the
shunt, the pads and the solder together, and the reading is high by their
resistance over the shunt's at every current
(<EqRef id="sense.pad_error" label="pad error" />). For example, pads and
solder of <Val example="bench-sense" name="R_pad" /> in the sense path of a
<Val example="bench-sense" name="R_SENSE" /> shunt make every reading
<Val example="bench-sense" name="eps_pad" /> high.

## How to confirm

Pass a known current through the shunt and measure the voltage twice: at
the ends of its pads, and at the shunt's own terminals (or its Kelvin pads).
The difference over the current is the pads' and the solder's resistance;
the ratio of the two voltages, minus one, is the reading's fractional error.

## Fix

Take the pads out of the sense path with a Kelvin connection: a
four-terminal shunt, or a two-terminal one on a four-pad (split-pad)
layout <Cite key="osullivan2012" />. The LTC6101 data sheet recommends
Kelvin connections to the sense resistor in all but the lowest-power
applications <Cite key="adi_ltc6101" where="Applications Information" />.
With a Kelvin connection, the pad resistance in the
[sense chain](../../design/sense-chain/) tool is zero.
