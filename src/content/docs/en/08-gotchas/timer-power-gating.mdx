## Symptom

A load whose supply a nano-power timer switches (the timer turns a power
switch on at intervals, and off again when the load signals that it has
finished) switches off right after it starts, runs only once, or behaves
differently from board to board.

## Why

Two of such a timer's inputs are easy to leave undefined. The TPL5110 is
the example here <Cite key="ti_tpl5110" />:

- Its EN/ONE_SHOT pin selects between periodic operation and a single
  cycle, and it must be tied to GND or VDD with short traces
  <Cite key="ti_tpl5110" />. A floating pin's level is undefined, and so is
  the mode.
- Its DONE input is the load's signal that it has finished. The timer takes
  a low-to-high transition as a valid DONE and switches the load off, and a
  pulse as short as 100 ns is enough <Cite key="ti_tpl5110" />. A stray
  rising edge that lasts that long ends the cycle just as a real DONE
  would: from a controller's pin, for example, while the controller starts
  up and before its firmware drives that pin.

## How to confirm

Watch DONE with an oscilloscope while the load powers up, triggered on a
rising edge, with a timebase short enough to show a pulse of the data
sheet's minimum width. A pulse there before the firmware signals that it
has finished is the cause. Check that the mode pin connects straight to
GND or VDD.

## Fix

Tie the mode pin to GND or VDD with a short trace <Cite key="ti_tpl5110" />.
Since a rising edge on DONE ends the cycle <Cite key="ti_tpl5110" />, hold
DONE low with a pull-down resistor, so that it stays low while nothing
drives it, and have the firmware raise it only when the work is done. Then
repeat the check above.
