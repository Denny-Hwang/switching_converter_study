## Goal

- Find, by experiment, the load resistance at which a buck and a boost enter DCM.
- Turn that resistance into the dimensionless $K$ and compare it with the critical value $K_\mathrm{crit}(D)$ of each converter.
- See what the output voltage does once the converter is in DCM.

## Before you start

- Read [CCM and DCM](../../02-theory/ccm-dcm/) and [the K parameter](../../02-theory/k-parameter/).
- Know the definition of $K$ (<EqRef id="K.def" label="the K parameter" />) and the critical values of the buck (<EqRef id="Kcrit.buck" label="buck" />) and the boost (<EqRef id="Kcrit.boost" label="boost" />).

## Steps

### 1. The buck's boundary

Open the simulator with the buck example:

<TrySim example="buck-basic" topology="buck" />

It starts in CCM with $R$ = <Val example="buck-basic" name="R" />. Raise $R$
step by step and watch the detected mode above the charts. It comes from the
simulated inductor current, which reaches zero within the period in DCM. It
is not computed from $K$. Close in on the resistance where the mode changes,
from both sides.

### 2. K at the boundary

With $L$ = <Val example="buck-basic" name="L" /> and $f_s$ =
<Val example="buck-basic" name="f_s" />, work out $K$ at the resistance you
found and enter it below. The simulator also prints $K$ and
$K_\mathrm{crit}$ next to the mode, so you can compare the equation's
boundary with the one the current shows.

### 3. The boost's boundary

Do the same with the boost example, which starts at
$R$ = <Val example="boost-ideal" name="R" />:

<TrySim example="boost-ideal" topology="boost" />

At the same duty ratio its critical value is much smaller than the buck's,
so it stays in CCM up to a much lighter load.

### 4. The output in DCM

Open the buck at a light load, $R$ = <Val example="buck-light-load" name="R" />:

<TrySim example="buck-light-load" topology="buck" />

Read the output voltage in the load table below the charts and enter it
below. It lies above $D V_g$, because in DCM the conversion ratio depends on
$K$ too (<EqRef id="buck.dcm.M" label="the buck's DCM ratio" />).

### 5. The boost's worst duty ratio

The boost's critical value is not monotonic in $D$. Sweep $D$ in the
equation explorer and find where it peaks:

## Gotchas

- $K$ rises with the switching frequency and the inductance and falls with the resistance, so changing $f_s$ or $L$ moves the boundary as much as changing the load does.
- Near the boundary the current only just touches zero, and the smallest change can flip the mode. Take the resistance halfway between the last CCM and the first DCM setting.
- In DCM the output depends on the load. Holding it at a set point then takes a different duty ratio, which a regulator finds by itself.
