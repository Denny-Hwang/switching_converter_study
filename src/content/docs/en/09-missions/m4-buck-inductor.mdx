## Goal

- Turn an inductor's specification into the core geometrical constant $K_g$ it needs, and choose a core.
- Finish the design in the magnetics designer: the fewest turns for the flux density, the gap for the inductance, and a winding that fits the window within its resistance budget.
- Find what fails first when the core is too small, and what it would take to make it work.

## Before you start

- Read the [design procedure](../../04-magnetics/design-procedure/) and the [magnetics designer](../../design/magnetics-designer/).
- Know where the specification comes from: the inductance and the peak current from the converter (the [buck](../../03-topologies/buck/) and the [converter designer](../../design/converter-designer/)), and $\Delta i$ as half the peak-to-peak ripple.

## Gotchas

- $B_\mathrm{max}$ is not the material's saturation flux density. It lies below it at the core's hottest, with a margin, as the magnetics designer's page explains.
- The turns follow from the flux density at the smallest cross-section, where it is highest, not at the effective area $A_e$.
- The gap estimate ignores fringing. A real gap must be longer for the same inductance, which is why data sheets give the $A_L$ of gapped sets.
- $\Delta i$ in the designer is half the peak-to-peak ripple. Entering the full swing doubles the flux swing, and the core loss it predicts grows more than that, as a power of the swing (<EqRef id="loss.steinmetz" label="core loss per volume" />).
