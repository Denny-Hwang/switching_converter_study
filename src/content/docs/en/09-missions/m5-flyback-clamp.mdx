## Goal

- Work out the voltages a flyback's switch and output diode block, and check the switch's in the simulator.
- Add leakage inductance and see why a clamp is needed at all.
- Size an RCD or a TVS clamp that leaves margin to the switch's rating, and state what it costs in dissipation and in the output voltage it allows without a load.

## Before you start

- Read the [flyback converter](../../03-topologies/flyback/), [leakage inductance](../../04-magnetics/leakage/) and [snubbers and clamps](../../04-magnetics/snubbers-clamps/).
- The turns ratio is 1:$n$ with $n$ the secondary turns over the primary turns, so the primary sees the output divided by $n$.

The simulator's transformer has no leakage inductance, so the switch voltage
is flat while the diode conducts.

### 3. The reflected voltage

With leakage, the leakage inductance still carries the peak primary current
when the switch opens, and it drives the drain above the input voltage plus
the reflected voltage until a clamp limits it <Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />. The clamp examples run the same
kind of flyback at a higher input voltage. Work out their reflected voltage
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_OR" label="reflected voltage" />): any clamp must hold
more than that.

### 4. The RCD clamp

Open the clamp check with the RCD example:

<TryTool tool="clamp-check" example="clamp-rcd" />

Enter the switch voltage it gives below. It leaves little margin to the
switch's rating of <Val example="clamp-rcd" name="V_rating" />. Change the
resistor until the margin is at least a tenth of the rating, and write down
the clamp loss at that value. The clamp trades its voltage against its loss
<Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />.

Open the TVS example at the same operating point:

load
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_ceiling" label="output ceiling set by the clamp" />).
Enter that ceiling below, and compare it with the RCD clamp's behaviour
without a load, which the tool warns about.

## Gotchas

- The reflected voltage includes the diode drop: the primary carries the output voltage plus the drop, divided by $n$.
- A clamp voltage close to the reflected voltage looks safe for the switch, but the leakage current then takes long to reset and the clamp absorbs much more than the leakage energy <Cite key="ti_ssztcv6" />. The tool warns when the reset would take a whole period.
- Check the switch at the highest input voltage and the highest peak current together, even if they never occur at once: it is conservative.
- An RCD clamp holds its voltage only while the output takes the power. Without a load its resistor must dissipate everything, and the voltages rise far above the values computed here (the clamp check's [output voltage without a load](../../design/clamp-check/#output-voltage-without-a-load)).
