## Goal

- Work out the power a linear source can give and the input resistance of a flyback in DCM with a fixed duty ratio.
- Choose the magnetizing inductance that matches the two, and check the bus voltage and the power at the match.
- Find the limits of the match: a mismatched duty ratio, CCM at a strong source, and the switch's rating.

## Before you start

- Read [source models](../../07-harvesting/source-models/), [matching](../../07-harvesting/matching/) and [the DCM flyback as a loss-free resistor](../../07-harvesting/lfr-dcm-flyback/).
- In DCM with a fixed duty ratio, the flyback's average input current is proportional to its input voltage: its input is a resistance that passes the power on instead of dissipating it <Cite key="singer1990" />.

## Steps

### 1. The source

The example source has an open-circuit voltage of <Val example="source-lfr" name="V_oc" />
behind <Val example="source-lfr" name="R_s" />. Work out the most power it
can deliver (<EqRef id="src.Pmax" label="maximum power from a linear source" />).

### 2. The flyback's input resistance

The flyback runs at $f_s$ = <Val example="source-lfr" name="f_s" /> and
$D$ = <Val example="source-lfr" name="D" />, with $L_M$ = <Val example="source-lfr" name="L_M" />.
Work out its input resistance (<EqRef id="lfr.R_in" label="the loss-free resistor" />)
and compare it with the source's.

### 3. Design for the match

Turn the question round: at this duty ratio and switching frequency, which
magnetizing inductance gives an input resistance equal to the source's
(<EqRef id="lfr.L_M" label="magnetizing inductance for a resistance" />)?

### 4. The bus at the match

The two resistances divide the source voltage
(<EqRef id="lfr.Vg" label="bus voltage of a loss-free resistor" />). Work out
the bus voltage, then open the source matcher with the example and read the
same numbers there:

<TryTool tool="source-matcher" example="source-lfr" />

### 5. A mismatched duty ratio

Double $D$ in the tool. Predict the new input resistance and the share of
the available power the flyback takes
(<EqRef id="lfr.eta" label="extraction by a loss-free resistor" />), then
compare with the tool.

### 6. Where the match ends

Set $D$ back and raise the source's open-circuit voltage. Find the amplitude
at which the bus reaches the critical voltage
(<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="critical input voltage" />) and CCM holds
it there. Read the switch voltage at that point against the switch's rating
of <Val example="source-lfr" name="V_rating" />.

## Gotchas

- The input resistance goes with the inverse square of the duty ratio: a small error in $D$ moves the match a lot.
- The flyback is a resistor only in DCM. Above the critical voltage CCM holds the bus, and the flyback becomes a constant-voltage sink whose share of the power depends on the source's amplitude.
- A larger power through the same resistance needs a higher bus voltage, and the switch blocks the bus plus the reflected output voltage. Check the rating at the strongest source.
