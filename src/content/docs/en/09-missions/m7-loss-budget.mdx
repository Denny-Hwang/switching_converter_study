## Goal

- Read a converter's losses by kind, across the load and the switching frequency, and say which one limits the efficiency where.
- Check one loss by hand against the tool.
- Plan the bench work that would confirm the budget: what to measure for each loss, with which instrument, and what limits the measurement.

## Before you start

- Read the [loss budget](../../design/loss-budget/) and the bench pages on [probes](../../06-bench/probes/), [current sensing](../../06-bench/current-sensing/) and [thermal design](../../06-bench/thermal/).
- The buck example converts <Val example="loss-buck" name="V_g" /> to <Val example="loss-buck" name="V" /> at <Val example="loss-buck" name="P" /> and <Val example="loss-buck" name="f_s" />.

## Steps

### 1. The budget

Open the loss budget with the buck example:

<TryTool tool="loss-budget" example="loss-buck" />

It simulates the converter at each load with the duty ratio adjusted until
the output is at its set value, and sorts the losses of the simulated period
by kind.

### 2. One loss by hand

The gate drive does not depend on the load. With a gate charge of
<Val example="loss-buck" name="Q_g" /> at a drive of <Val example="loss-buck" name="V_GS" />,
work it out (<EqRef id="loss.gate" label="gate-drive power" />) and compare
it with the tool's bucket. Enter it below. The driver and the gate
resistances dissipate it, not the switch.

### 3. Full load and light load

Name the largest loss at full load and at a tenth of the load, and say why
they differ: which losses follow the square of the current
(<EqRef id="loss.cond" label="conduction loss" />), and which are set by the
switching frequency alone.

### 4. The switching frequency

Double $f_s$ and watch each bucket and the efficiency. Say which buckets
doubled, and what the higher frequency buys in return (a smaller ripple for
the same inductance, or a smaller inductance for the same ripple).

### 5. What the tool leaves out

The simulated transitions are instantaneous, so the overlap loss of a
hard-switched transistor is not in the budget
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 4" />, and the core loss is an estimate
from coefficients measured with sinusoidal excitation
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />. Decide how your plan would see
both.

### 6. The bench plan

For each loss, write down the quantity you would measure, the instrument,
and the limit that matters: the probe's rise time against the switching
edges (<EqRef id="probe.t_rise" label="probe rise time" />), the burden and
the pad resistance of a shunt, a meter that averages a pulsed current, the
temperature rise of a part. End with how you would measure the efficiency
itself.

## Gotchas

- The efficiency at full load hides the light-load picture. Losses that do not fall with the load dominate there, so read the whole curve.
- A sense chain that clips on the peaks of a pulsed current reads a lower average than a meter across the same shunt ([a current reading below the meter](../../08-gotchas/dmm-true-average/)).
- A probe with a long ground lead rings with its own capacitance and adds its own edge to the one you measure ([probes](../../06-bench/probes/)).
