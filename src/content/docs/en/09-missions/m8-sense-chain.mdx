## Goal

- Work out a sense chain's burden, its offset-equivalent current and its filter corner.
- Find why the example misses its error target at the smallest current, and fix it.
- Keep the output below the amplifier's largest output and the ADC's full scale at the largest current, in the worst case of the offset and the pad resistance.

## Before you start

- Read the [sense chain](../../design/sense-chain/) and [current sensing](../../06-bench/current-sensing/).
- Know the two gotchas this mission is about: [pad and solder resistance](../../08-gotchas/shunt-pad-resistance/) and [a current-output amplifier's headroom](../../08-gotchas/current-output-headroom/).

### 3. Why the low end fails

At the smallest current, add the pad error
(<EqRef id="sense.pad_error" label="pad error" />) and the offset
(<EqRef id="sense.rel_error" label="relative error" />). Say which of the two

### 4. The fix, and what it costs

Change the chain until the tool reports the error at the smallest current
within the target: the connection, the shunt or the amplifier's offset. Then
check the largest current: the worst-case output must stay below the
amplifier's largest output and the ADC's full scale. If it does not, lower
the gain. Write down what your fix costs in burden and dissipation.

### 5. The filter

Work out the filter's corner (<EqRef id="rc.fc" label="R-C corner frequency" />)
and compare it with the Nyquist frequency of the ADC's sampling rate
(<EqRef id="adc.nyquist" label="Nyquist frequency" />). Enter the corner
below.

## Gotchas

- A current-output amplifier's output cannot swing to its supply. Its headroom sets the largest output whatever the gain ([headroom](../../08-gotchas/current-output-headroom/)).
- The data sheet gives the offset's largest magnitude, not its sign: check both signs, the one that raises the output at the top and the one that lowers the reading at the bottom.
- With a current-output amplifier, the filter's capacitor sees $R_\mathrm{OUT}$ in series with $R_f$ (<EqRef id="sense.filter_R" label="the filter's resistance" />), so the corner is lower than $R_f$ alone gives.
- A single pole below the Nyquist frequency still passes part of the signal there. Read the filter's gain at the Nyquist frequency, and at the switching frequency if you enter it.
