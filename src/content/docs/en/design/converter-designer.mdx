Pick a topology and give the input-voltage range, the output voltage, the
full-load power, the lightest load that must stay in CCM, the switching
frequency and two ripple targets. The designer then gives:

- the duty-ratio range;
- the inductance for the current-ripple target and the one that keeps the
  lightest load in CCM;
- the output capacitance for the voltage-ripple target;
- the ripple, the peak current and the switch and diode voltages at the
  worst point of the input range;
- $K$ against $K_\mathrm{crit}$ over the duty-ratio range.

Each result names the catalogue equation it comes from. The links under the
results open the [simulator](../../simulate/simulator/) with the designed parts
at either end of the input range, so you can check the design in the time
domain; the parts there are ideal, apart from the flyback's diode drop. Other
links open the [loss budget](../loss-budget/) at the same points, with the
switch and diode data of its synthetic example and no core data, because the
designer does not size the core.

## How it sizes the converter

The designer examines a grid of input voltages across the range, plus the
points inside the range where a quantity peaks, and keeps the worst case of
each result. It uses only catalogue equations. Where it needs an equation
solved for one of its inputs (the duty ratio, an inductance, a capacitance),
it finds that value by bisection on the catalogue's own evaluator, so the
tool and the theory pages cannot disagree.

1. **Duty ratio.** The CCM conversion ratio is solved for $D$ at each input
   voltage: <EqRef id="buck.ccm.M" label="buck" />,
   <EqRef id="boost.ccm.M" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="buckboost.ccm.M" label="buck-boost" />,
   <EqRef id="flyback.ccm.M" label="flyback" /> and
   <EqRef id="forward.ccm.M" label="forward" />. For the flyback the diode drop
   adds to the output voltage, as in the volt-second balance behind
   <EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="the critical input voltage" />.
2. **Inductance for the ripple target.** At full load the average inductor
   current (<EqRef id="buck.IL" label="buck" />,
   <EqRef id="boost.IL" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="buckboost.IL" label="buck-boost" />, and the flyback's
   <EqRef id="flyback.IM" label="magnetizing current" />) times the ripple
   target gives the allowed ripple, and the ripple equation
   (<EqRef id="buck.ripple.iL" label="buck" />,
   <EqRef id="boost.ripple.iL" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="buckboost.ripple.iL" label="buck-boost" />,
   <EqRef id="flyback.ripple.iM" label="flyback" />,
   <EqRef id="forward.ripple.iL" label="forward" />) is solved for the
   inductance. The forward converter's output stage is a buck converter fed
   with $n V_g$, so its output inductor carries the load current
   <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />.
3. **Inductance for CCM at the lightest load.** The
   <EqRef id="L.crit" label="critical inductance" /> at the lightest load,
   with the largest $K_\mathrm{crit}$ over the duty-ratio range
   (<EqRef id="Kcrit.buck" label="buck and forward" />,
   <EqRef id="Kcrit.boost" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="Kcrit.buckboost" label="buck-boost" />,
   <EqRef id="Kcrit.flyback" label="flyback" />). For the flyback the diode
   drop again adds to the output voltage, at the same output current, so the
   load here is the one that draws that current at the output voltage plus
   the diode drop; with the load itself, the boundary would come out too low.
4. **Inductance used.** The larger of the two, unless you enter one. With it,
   $K$ (<EqRef id="K.def" label="definition" />) at full load and at the
   lightest load (for the flyback, with the load of step 3) is compared with
   $K_\mathrm{crit}$ in the chart: the converter is in CCM where $K$ lies
   above the curve.
5. **Ripple and stresses.** The ripple with the inductance used, the
   <EqRef id="ripple.Ipk" label="peak current" />, and the switch voltage
   (<EqRef id="buck.Vds" label="buck" />, <EqRef id="boost.Vds" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="buckboost.Vds" label="buck-boost" />,
   <EqRef id="flyback.Vds_off" label="flyback" />,
   <EqRef id="forward.Vds" label="forward" />). In the buck, boost and
   buck-boost the diode blocks the same voltage as the switch
   <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />; the flyback's diode blocks
   <EqRef id="flyback.diode.VR" label="its own reverse voltage" />.
6. **Output capacitance.** The voltage-ripple equation is solved for $C$:
   <EqRef id="buck.ripple.v" label="buck and forward" />,
   <EqRef id="boost.ripple.v" label="boost" />,
   <EqRef id="buckboost.ripple.v" label="buck-boost" />,
   <EqRef id="flyback.ripple.v" label="flyback" />.

For the forward converter, the tool also checks the duty ratio against the
<EqRef id="forward.reset.Dmax" label="reset limit" />.

## Ripple targets

Both targets are ratios of the **half** peak-to-peak ripple, the convention
of the catalogue <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 2" />: the current target
is the inductor-current ripple over the average inductor current at full load
(the magnetizing current for the flyback), and the voltage target is the
output-voltage ripple over the output voltage.

## Limitations

- **Ideal parts.** Switches, diodes (except the flyback's diode drop),
  inductors and capacitors are ideal. A capacitor's series resistance adds a
  ripple of its own (<EqRef id="cap.esr.ripple" label="ESR ripple" />), so the
  capacitance found here is a lower bound.
- **CCM designs only.** The duty ratios and ripples assume CCM, and the
  inductance is sized for CCM; a converter designed to run in DCM (a DCM
  flyback, for example) is not sized here. Where the full load runs in DCM,
  the designer says so, and the DCM equations apply there instead.
- **Not sized here.** Ratings and thermal limits are not checked, and neither
  are the forward converter's magnetizing inductance and reset winding. Check
  the design in the simulator, and estimate its losses with the
  [loss budget](../loss-budget/).
