Give the operating point and the data of the switch, the diode and the
inductor or transformer core, all in SI units. The loss budget simulates the
converter at ten loads and nine switching frequencies. At each point it
adjusts the duty ratio until the output is at its set value, then applies
the catalogue's loss equations to the simulated currents. It shows:

- the losses at full load, each with the equations behind it;
- the losses as stacked bars against the load and against the switching
  frequency, with the efficiency.

The link under the table opens the full-load point in the
[simulator](../../simulate/simulator/), non-ideal parts included.

## How it computes the losses

At each point the load resistance follows from the output voltage and the
power. The duty ratio starts at the value of the ideal converter, in CCM or
in DCM depending on $K$ against $K_\mathrm{crit}$
(<EqRef id="K.def" label="K" />). A search then adjusts it until the
simulated output is within one part in a thousand of its set value: secant
steps, kept between the tries that gave an output below and above the set
value, and halving that interval when a step would leave it. If no duty
ratio reaches the set value, the tool keeps the closest try and says so. The
forward converter's duty ratio stops at its
<EqRef id="forward.reset.Dmax" label="reset limit" />. The losses of the
simulated period are then sorted into buckets:

- **Conduction.** <EqRef id="loss.cond" label="Resistive loss" /> of the rms
  switch current in $R_\mathrm{on}$ and of the rms inductor current in the
  winding resistance $R_L$ <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 3" />.
- **Capacitive switching.** The switch discharges the node capacitance at
  every turn-on (<EqRef id="loss.sw.cap" label="capacitive turn-on loss" />),
  from the simulated switch voltage just before turn-on, which includes any
  DCM ringing.
- **Gate drive.** The <EqRef id="loss.gate" label="gate-charge loss" />, which
  the driver and the gate resistances dissipate, not the switch channel.
- **Core.** The flux density follows the inductor current, so half the
  current swing gives the ac flux amplitude
  (<EqRef id="mag.B_pk" label="flux density from the current" />). The
  <EqRef id="loss.steinmetz" label="Steinmetz equation" /> gives the loss per
  unit volume, and the <EqRef id="loss.core" label="core volume" /> the total.
  The tool also shows the peak flux density at the peak current, from the
  same equation, to be checked against the core's saturation flux density;
  it does not check it itself.
- **Diode.** The <EqRef id="loss.diode" label="diode loss" /> from the
  simulated average and rms diode current.
- **Leakage energy** (flyback). The energy stored in the leakage inductance at
  the peak primary current (<EqRef id="flyback.leak.E" label="leakage energy" />)
  is lost once per period (<EqRef id="flyback.leak.P" label="leakage power" />).
  A dissipative clamp also absorbs energy from the reflected voltage, so this
  is a lower bound for its loss.

$R_\mathrm{on}$, $R_L$, $V_F$ and the node capacitance are also in the
simulated circuit, so the currents include their effect. The diode's slope
resistance, the gate drive, the core and the leakage inductance are not: their
losses are computed from the simulated currents. The efficiency is the output
power over the output power plus all the buckets.

## Limitations

- **No overlap loss.** The simulated transitions are instantaneous, so the
  loss while a hard-switched transistor's voltage and current overlap during
  a transition is not included <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 4" />.
- **Core loss is an estimate.** The Steinmetz coefficients describe
  sinusoidal excitation <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 10" />, and the
  converter's winding voltage is rectangular.
- **One winding resistance, one core.** $R_L$ is in series with the
  inductance in every interval. For the flyback it is the winding resistance
  referred to the primary: the primary's while the switch is on, the
  secondary's divided by $n^2$ while the diode conducts, one value for both
  <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 6" />. The core data belong to the
  inductor, or to the flyback's transformer; the forward converter's
  transformer core and windings are not included.
- **No thermal model.** The resistances do not rise with temperature.
