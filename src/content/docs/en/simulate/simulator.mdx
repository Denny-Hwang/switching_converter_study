Pick a topology or a synthetic preset and set the parameters in SI units.
The simulator then computes one period of the periodic steady state and
shows:

- the inductor, diode, switch and output waveforms;
- the conduction mode it detects, with $K$ and $K_\mathrm{crit}$;
- the simulated values next to the catalogue's equations at the same
  operating point;
- the losses and the efficiency computed from the waveforms.

Everything runs in your browser with the same `pe-core` engine that the test
suite validates. The URL holds the whole state, and the "Try it" links on the
learning pages open the simulator with a preset.

## What is modelled

Each converter is a set of linear circuits, one for each interval of the
switching period: switch on, switch off with the diode conducting, and, in
DCM, the idle interval <Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 2" />,
<Cite key="erickson2020" where="Ch. 5" />.

- **Switches and diodes.** The transistor is an ideal switch with an optional
  on-resistance. The diode is a constant forward drop that blocks ideally,
  with no reverse recovery. The inductor can have a winding resistance.
- **Transformers.** They are ideal (1:n with $n$ the secondary turns over
  the primary turns) except for the magnetizing inductance. There is no
  leakage inductance.
- **Transitions are instantaneous.** An optional capacitance across the
  switch makes the idle interval of DCM ring
  (<EqRef id="dcm.ring.f" label="ringing frequency" />). The switch's body
  diode clamps the ringing at zero switch voltage, and the energy in the
  capacitance is lost at every turn-on
  (<EqRef id="loss.sw.cap" label="capacitive turn-on loss" />).
- **Source-driven mode.** The input can be a Thevenin source charging a bus
  capacitor instead of a fixed voltage. With a fixed output and a fixed duty
  ratio, this reproduces the constant-voltage sink of the flyback
  (<EqRef id="flyback.V_crit" label="the pinned input voltage" />).

## How it computes

- **Within an interval** the state (inductor current, capacitor voltages)
  obeys a linear differential equation. The simulator advances it with the
  exact solution of that equation (a matrix exponential) over sub-steps of
  one two-thousandth of the switching period.
- **Events** such as the diode current reaching zero or a clamp starting to
  conduct are located inside a sub-step by bisection on the same exact
  solution. The gate edges fall exactly at the start of the period and at the
  duty ratio.
- **The periodic steady state** is found by Newton iteration on the map from
  one period's initial state to the next. It is accepted when the largest
  change of any state over one period, relative to that state's peak, is
  below one part in a million, within at most two thousand periods.
- **The mode** is DCM when the inductor current stays at zero for part of
  the period, BCM when it only touches zero, and CCM otherwise.

## Validation

The test suite (`packages/pe-core/test/sim.test.ts`) checks the simulator
against the equations:

- **Validation grid.** For the buck, boost, buck-boost and flyback, over a
  grid of duty ratios and values of $K$ on both sides of $K_\mathrm{crit}$:
  - the simulated conversion ratio and peak-to-peak inductor ripple are
    within 2 % of the equations;
  - the detected mode matches $K$ against $K_\mathrm{crit}$.
- **Energy balance.** With losses, the input energy per period equals the
  output energy plus the computed losses.
- **Ringing.** The DCM ringing frequency matches the L-C equation.
- **Source-driven flyback.** It pins its input at the critical voltage when
  the source can supply more than the DCM power there, and it settles below
  that voltage in DCM when the source cannot.

## Limitations

- **No transition shapes.** There is no leakage inductance, reverse
  recovery or switching transition, so the simulator shows no turn-off
  spikes and no overlap loss.
- **Fixed duty ratio.** There is no control loop and no core loss.
- **A periodic steady state must exist.** A fixed output fed from a fixed
  input in CCM, for example, has none, because the inductor current grows
  without bound. The status line then reports that no steady state was
  reached.
